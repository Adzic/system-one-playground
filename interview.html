The Interview Room

Seven questions in the manner of Ilya Sutskever's interviews, rebuilt for data scientists. Reconstructions, not quotations: the method is the documented one, a single simple question followed by why, five levels down.

The gym's problems are simulations. This page is the opposite. Answer each question out loud, or in your head, before you open the ladder. Reading the ladder first is cheating yourself.

QUESTION 1

Your model scores 95 in validation and 90 in production. Where did the five points go?

THE LADDER

He is listening for whether you reach for one cause or enumerate the real ones: leakage, distribution shift, selection on the validation set. And whether you can say how you would prove which one it is, not just name it.

QUESTION 2

You looked at the test set three times while building this. How much do you trust the number now, and how much less than if you had looked once?

THE LADDER

The follow up is the test. Can you put a rough magnitude on adaptive overfitting, or do you just say "it is contaminated" and stop? Trust is a quantity here, not a vibe.

QUESTION 3

Two models, same AUC. One has far better log loss. Which ships, and what is log loss actually measuring?

THE LADDER

He wants the bits answer: log loss is a code length, an expected number of bits to encode the true labels, and it punishes confident wrongness in a way ranking metrics forgive. Then: when does that matter for the decision the model feeds?

QUESTION 4

Your classifier says 0.8. What does that sentence mean, and how would you check whether it is true?

THE LADDER

Calibration, stated plainly. Most candidates can define it; the ladder goes to reliability curves, base rates, and what quietly breaks calibration in production data.

QUESTION 5

When would you not trust cross validation?

THE LADDER

One line, and it finds everyone who has ever leaked. He is listening for grouped data, time structure, preprocessing fit on the full set, and the same person or member appearing in both train and test.

QUESTION 6

You double the training data and nothing improves. Name the possible reasons, and how you would tell them apart.

THE LADDER

Noise floor, label quality, capacity, wrong features, optimization. The separating experiments are the answer; the list is just the door.

QUESTION 7

Why does averaging a hundred trees beat one tree, and when does it stop helping?

THE LADDER

Everyone can say variance reduction. He keeps going until you hit correlation between the trees as the binding constraint, which is where the recitation usually ends.

What is not here

Notice what is absent: no frameworks, no library questions, nothing about this year's tooling.

Every question can be answered at a whiteboard by someone who understands their own daily work, and every one has a floor that drops out if you only know the vocabulary.
